# Calibration

Generated by `scripts/calibrate.mjs`. The model runs through history (prices from
the OECD ratio, flat before 1970, held at the last year after it); its 2019-20 is
compared with ABS's.

- Saving rate: **4%** of income before 65 (fitted).
- Housing share: **32%** of income: the most a mortgage's repayments may take (fitted). For comparison,
  ABS housing costs ÷ disposable income by age band: 15–24 24%, 25–34 19%, 35–44 18%, 45–54 14%, 55–64 11%, 65–74 12%, 75+ 14%.
- Giving share: **4.57%** of income, once owned outright, to the child
  (pinned). Model gifts 2017–2019: $1,215 per household a year;
  PC: $1,214 ($11.8B from parents in 2018-19 ÷ 9,732,400 households).
- Real mortgage rate: 3.64% (see VALIDATION.md).

Where it misses, and why (not tuned away): the model's households buy once, at the
mean price, on one income decile all their lives. Real first buyers often start
cheaper and trade up later (stretching mortgages), and incomes move between deciles.

## Inheritances (cross-check, not fitted)

Model estates passed down, 2016–2020: $8,195 per household a year.
PC estimates of intergenerational inheritance outflow, 2018 (figure 1.7): $52B (HILDA)
to $70B (administrative), i.e. $5,347–$7,165 per household.
The model passes whole estates down one line at exactly 90, with no surviving spouse,
aged care or other heirs, so it runs high.
