# Calibration

Generated by `scripts/calibrate.mjs`. The model runs through history (prices from
the OECD ratio, flat before 1970, held at the last year after it); its 2019-20 is
compared with ABS's.

- Saving rate: **8%** of income before 65 (fitted).
- Lending limit: **18%** of income, the most a loan's repayments may take (fitted).
  The model tests repayments at the real rate; a bank tests the first year's repayments at the
  nominal rate (on average 8.4%), which are 1.74× larger: in those terms the limit
  is about 31% of income, close to the usual 30% test.
- Giving share: **4.77%** of income, once owned outright, to the child
  (pinned). Model gifts 2017–2019: $1,213 per household a year;
  PC: $1,214 ($11.8B from parents in 2018-19 ÷ 9,732,400 households;
  HILDA counts people and all gifts from parents, not only help with housing).
- Real mortgage rate: 3.31% (rates actually paid, less inflation; see VALIDATION.md).

ABS renting here includes its "other" tenure (2–6%). Where the model misses, and why
(not tuned away): its households stay in one income decile all their lives, so the
lowest two rows never buy; and it has no 20–24-year-old buyers.

## Checks (not fitted)

- First home bought 2010–2020: median age 31 (n = 27).
- Estates passed down, 2016–2020: $9,230 per household a year. PC, 2018
  (figure 1.7): $52B estimated from HILDA, $5,347 per household;
  Roach (2018) as scaled by the PC, which it says tends to inflate the value: $70B.
  The model passes each estate whole to one heir at exactly 90, with no surviving spouse,
  aged care or other heirs, so it runs high.
- Heirs' ages, 1990–2020: 6 grandchildren (about 40) and 105
  children (about 65). The PC finds most inheritances go to people in their 50s and early
  60s; the model's 25-year generations and death at 90 put them at 65 or 40.
