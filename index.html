Passing it back

Each dot is a household, ageing left to right from 20 to 90 as the years run from 1970. Rows are income deciles, highest at the top; a dot's size is its wealth. Households rent and save, buy when they can, and pay it off. Owners' wealth then rises with house prices, and they pass ● help to their child (same row, 25 years younger). At 90, their ● estate passes down: to the child, or past them to the grandchild if the child already owns outright.

What's real and what's assumed

  • Incomes are ABS household disposable incomes by decile and age, 2019-20 (Household Income and Wealth, table 10.1). A household stays in its decile all its life, and so does its family line.
  • House prices start from the ABS mean dwelling price, $685k in 2019-20 (ABS publishes a mean, not a median). Each year's price is that, scaled by the year's price-to-income ratio from the OECD, so prices are at 2019-20 incomes: $376k in 1985, $818k in 2024. Before 1970 the price is held at the 1970 level. After 2025 it is held at the 2025 level, as a what-if, not a forecast. A house is always worth this year's price, so owners' wealth rises with it.
  • Mortgages: a 30-year loan at a real rate of 3.6%. That's the RBA standard variable rate from 1970 to 2025, 8.8% on average, minus inflation. A household buys once it has a 20% deposit and the repayments fit within 32% of its income.
  • Calibrated: the 4% saving rate (before 65) and the 32% lending limit are fitted so the model's 2019-20 home ownership by age matches ABS's. Owners outright give 4.6% of income to their child. That's set so the gifts total the Productivity Commission's figure for gifts from parents, about $1,200 per household a year.
  • Checked, not fitted: estates passed down come to about $8,200 per household a year in the model, against the PC's estimate of $5,300–$7,200 for 2018. It runs high because there is no surviving spouse, aged care or other heirs. The model also has too few young owners, since real first buyers start cheaper and trade up.
  • Assumptions: each child is 25 years younger than their parent, in the same row. Everyone dies at 90 and spends their savings down from 65. An heir who already owns outright passes the estate on to their own child. There is no super, shares or second property.
